How stacked discounts work

Each extra discount applies to the already reduced price. For $100 with 30% off and then an extra 20% off: $100 × 0.70 = $70, then $70 × 0.80 = $56. You save 44%, not 50%.

Example: 20% off plus 15% off on $100 → $100 × 0.80 = $80, then $80 × 0.85 = $68 (32% total off, not 35%).

Formula: final = price × (1 − d1) × (1 − d2) × … − fixed coupons (fixed amounts subtract at the step you place them).

FAQ

Is 20% off plus 20% off the same as 40% off?

No. It equals 36% off, because the second 20% is taken from the price that is already 20% lower.

What is coupon stacking?

Coupon stacking means using more than one discount on the same order — for example a sitewide sale percent plus a promo code, or a membership extra % after a clearance price. Stores decide which codes can combine; this calculator only shows the math if they do stack in the order you enter.

Can I stack a percentage sale with a dollar-off coupon?

Often yes when the retailer allows it, but the order of discounts changes the final price (see rows C vs D above). Enter the steps in the same sequence as the checkout screen for a sequential discount estimate.

Does the order of percentage discounts matter?

For percentages only, no: multiplication gives the same result in any order. Order matters once a fixed-amount coupon (like $10 off) is involved. Taking $10 off before a percentage discount usually saves less than taking the percentage first, then $10 off.

Should tax be added before or after discounts?

In most US states sales tax is calculated on the discounted price. Check your local rules; this tool applies tax after all discounts.
