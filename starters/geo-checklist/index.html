GEO vs AEO vs classic SEO (plain vocabulary)

These labels overlap. You do not pick one and abandon the others. You reuse crawlability, entities, and clear writing—then you change what you measure and how you structure claims so machines can quote them safely.

Classic SEO

Ranked web results

Surface: blue links, snippets, classic SERPs.

Success signal: impressions, clicks, rankings you can defend in Search Console.

Typical moves: intent match, technical health, links earned honestly, on-page clarity.

AEO

Answer-style surfaces

Surface: featured / answer-first UI where a short answer sits above the list.

Success signal: being the cited or paraphrased source in that answer box—when it exists.

Typical moves: direct answers up top, FAQ blocks, clear headings machines can lift.

GEO

Generative answers

Surface: AI Overviews and other generative summaries that synthesize many sources.

Success signal: accurate brand/page representation when models answer—checked manually, not a vendor “official score.”

Typical moves: citable claims, entity consistency, schema where it fits, answer-first sections.

What “good” looks like without fake metrics

Search engines and AI vendors do not sell you a public, official GEO score or guaranteed citation rate. Prefer observable proxies you can explain to a teammate:

  • Important URLs return 200 for people and known crawlers; no accidental blocks of legitimate bots you care about.
  • The first screen answers the query in plain language before the pitch.
  • Brand, product, and author names stay consistent across pages and profiles.
  • Structured data matches visible content (FAQ, Article, HowTo only when the page really is that type).
  • You keep a simple log of AI-answer spot checks plus Search Console—not a screenshot of a made-up “citation %.”

Google traffic pages ≠ ChatGPT traffic pages

Users who grew up on Google tend to search with keywords. Users who start in chat tend to ask in a full sentence with constraints. Plan the page for the way that person asks.

In practice, tutorial / guide URLs often win classic search clicks. Tool / form / generator URLs are more likely to show up as AI-assistant referrals. Treat them as two page types, not one SEO plan. Sample observation from practitioners — not a traffic forecast.

  • Do not estimate AI referral volume from Google Search Console clicks or impressions.
  • Keep writing articles for Google. Build an operable tool page when you want AI-assistant visits.
  • Ship the page, then the files. Checks for llms.txt, schema, and the sitemap live on the AEO checklist.

Agency vs DIY

Decision line: DIY with a checklist (and later, product tools) when you ship content weekly and can own technical fixes; hire help when you need strategy across many sites, remediation you cannot staff, or a second pair of eyes on brand risk—not because someone sold you a mysterious GEO score.

When it fits Indie publishers, small content teams, learning GEO vocabulary Multi-brand, compliance-heavy, or no in-house SEO time

FAQ

What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)?
GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means improving how your brand and pages show up inside AI-generated answers and summaries—not only how they rank in classic blue-link results. It overlaps with strong SEO technicals and clear, citable writing; it is not a separate license or an official “GEO score” sold by search engines.
How is GEO different from AEO and classic SEO?
Classic SEO optimizes for ranked web results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) focuses on winning featured/answer-style surfaces. GEO emphasizes visibility and accurate representation inside generative answers. In practice you reuse crawlability, entities, and answer-first content; you change how you measure success and how you structure claims for machines to quote.
What belongs on a GEO / AI SEO checklist?
Prioritize crawl/access for people and bots, answer-first sections with consistent entities, appropriate structured data, visible expertise signals, and a measurement routine you can explain (manual AI-answer checks plus Search Console)—not a promised official citation rate.
Will this checklist guarantee AI citations or traffic?
No. It is a prioritization aid for indie publishers. Results depend on your niche, competition, and product quality. Anyone promising guaranteed generative citations is selling certainty they cannot own.
Is grey-hat cloaking or bot-only content part of AI SEO?
No. Show the same honest content to users and crawlers. Shortcuts that deceive systems create trust debt you will pay later.
